The dispute in one paragraph

Cassidy Group Investments Ltd (claimant) sued Phoenix Life Ltd / abrdn (defendant) over “Project Lock” — a proposed institutional forward-funding of an 829-bed purpose-built student accommodation scheme at Stafford Street, Wolverhampton (development cashflow ≈ £86.99m). From roughly August 2021 to mid-2022 Phoenix and its advisers engaged with Cassidy on terms, indemnities, due diligence, an insurance wrap and a funding facility. Cassidy says it was induced to run up substantial abortive/reliance costs and then Phoenix walked away; Phoenix says the deal was always conditional and Cassidy failed to deliver the deliverables needed to close.

Cassidy’s case (why the emails should score “for”)

  • Phoenix repeatedly signalled it was keen to close with “no show-stoppers”, keeping Cassidy in the deal.
  • Cassidy flagged its reliance and exposure early (e.g. £1,000,000 LOI to Winvic; ~£2m minimum exposure) — Phoenix knew.
  • Phoenix never produced core finance documents — no facility letter — and let the Ageas / Lime Risk insurance wrap stall.
  • Internal Phoenix/abrdn correspondence suggests they knew the insurance policy would not be accepted yet pressed on.
  • Requirements shifted over ~10 months (“institutional capital-markets approach”, “cashflow not property deal”).

Phoenix’s defence (why some emails score “against”)

  • Express “on-risk until funding is finalised” language — nothing was assured until close.
  • Consistent narrative that Cassidy didn’t deliver: no credible Knight Frank Market Demand Report, no audited/workable cashflow model, no signed Sodexo FM agreement, no certified lifecycle costs.
  • Third-party/adviser frustration that Cassidy wasn’t engaging (e.g. refusing face-to-face).

The seven disclosure issues

    How an email becomes a score

    Every email is turned into a signed Case Impact Score (CIS) — positive = helps Cassidy, negative = helps Phoenix:

    sign = +1 (Support) · −1 (Adverse) · 0 (Neutral)
    base = sign × supportScore  (strength 1–10)
    bias = if For → base × B   if Against → base ÷ B
    CIS = bias × issueWeight × admissionBonus

    Deliberately biased toward Cassidy. The Cassidy Bias (B) amplifies supporting evidence and discounts adverse evidence. B = 1.0 is objective; higher tilts the whole picture in Cassidy’s favour — exactly the advocacy lens the team asked for. Everything is transparent and adjustable.
